# Common Expression

## Starting class

- *Alright, let's get started.* / *Let's dive in.*
- *Quick recap of where we left off.*
- *Before we begin, a couple of announcements.*
- *Can everyone see the back of the room?* (= is my slide readable)

## Moving through material

- *Bear with me for a second.* (something is loading / he's finding his place)
- *Let me back up a bit.*
- *This is the key takeaway.* / *If you remember one thing from today...*
- *We'll come back to this later.*
- *I'm going to hand-wave this part.* (= skip the derivation — very common in systems and theory courses)
- *This is beyond the scope of this course.*
- *Intuitively, what's happening here is...*

## Checking comprehension

- *Does that make sense?* — the most frequent sentence in American teaching. Note it's not really a question; silence counts as yes.
- *Any questions so far?*
- *Am I losing anyone?*
- *Show of hands — who's seen this before?*
- *Feel free to jump in.* (= interrupt me, it's allowed)

## Answering questions — the polite-disagreement layer

- *That's a great question.* — often means "I need three seconds to think."
- *Say more about that.* / *Can you unpack that?* — he wants you to elaborate, not repeat.
- *I think you're conflating two things.* — you mixed up two concepts. Direct correction.
- *Not quite — but you're on the right track.* — you're wrong, keep going.
- *Let's take that offline.* — good question, wrong time; come to office hours.
- *I'd have to think about that.* — genuine uncertainty, and a good sign. Professors here say this without embarrassment.

## Logistics

- *This is due by end of day Friday.* (EOD usually means 11:59pm)
- *I'll post the slides after class.*
- *Office hours are Tuesday two to four, or by appointment.*
- *No extensions unless you talk to me beforehand.*
- *This will be on the exam* — said rarely, and always worth writing down.

## Grad seminars and talks — your daily environment

- *Let's go around the room and introduce ourselves.*
- *Who wants to lead the discussion this week?*
- *I'll play devil's advocate here.* (= I'm arguing a position I may not hold)
- *What's the contribution?* — the single most important question in a paper discussion. Not "what did they do" but "what's new."
- *Does the evaluation actually support the claim?*
- *That's orthogonal to the point.* (= unrelated; common in CS specifically)
- *Let's table that for now.* (US usage = postpone. In British English it means the opposite, so don't trust your instinct here.)
- *I'm not sure I follow — can you walk me through it?*

## Introducing a concept

- *X is essentially Y* — the standard opener for a simplification
- *Think of X as Y* — signals an analogy is coming, not a definition
- *At a high level, X does Y* (then: *now let's zoom in*)
- *The idea here is that...*
- *Roughly speaking* / *loosely speaking* — flags that precision is being sacrificed on purpose
- *Formally, X is defined as...* — the register flips to Latinate here

## Mechanism — how it works

- *under the hood* (= internally) — *under the hood, it's just a linked list*
- *boils down to* — *it boils down to a lookup* (핵심[본질]이 …이다)
- *falls back to* — *if the cache misses, it falls back to main memory*
- *kicks in* — *the scheduler kicks in every tick* (효과가 나타나기 시작하다)
- *hands off to* / *delegates to*
- *short-circuits* — skips the rest once the answer is known
- *X is backed by Y* — *the map is backed by a hash table*
- *X sits on top of Y* — layering
- *X wraps Y* — X is a thin layer over Y

## Trade-offs — the most-used frame in systems

- *There's no free lunch.*
- *You're trading X for Y.* / *at the cost of*
- *X buys you Y.* — *batching buys you throughput*
- *It's a wash.* (= the two cancel out, no net gain)
- *Pathological case* / *degenerate case* / *worst case*
- *X doesn't scale.*
- *X is the bottleneck.*

## Precision qualifiers — say these and you sound like an insider

- *modulo X* — *it's correct, modulo the locking* (= setting aside X)
- *orthogonal to* — independent, changing one doesn't affect the other
- *by construction* — true because of how it was built, no proof needed
- *without loss of generality (WLOG)*
- *up to X* — *unique up to renaming*
- *in the limit* / *asymptotically*
- *amortized* — averaged over a sequence, not per operation

## Dismissing or deferring

- *hand-wave* — skip the rigor deliberately
- *That's a red herring.*
- *It's not the interesting part.*
- *strictly speaking* — usually precedes a correction to something just said
